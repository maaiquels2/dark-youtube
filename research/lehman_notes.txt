- 1850: Lehman Brothers founded in Montgomery, Alabama, by brothers Henry, Emanuel and Mayer Lehman as a dry-goods and cotton business; later moved to New York and became the fourth-largest U.S. investment bank. https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/lehman-brothers
- 1994: Lehman spun off from American Express as a public company under CEO Richard S. Fuld Jr., who led it until the collapse. https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/lehman-brothers
- 2006-2007: Lehman expanded aggressively into subprime and commercial real estate; by end-2007 its leverage was about 31 to 1. https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-FCIC/pdf/GPO-FCIC.pdf
- Bankruptcy examiner Anton R. Valukas (Jenner & Block, report Mar 11, 2010) found Lehman used "Repo 105" transactions to temporarily remove about $50 billion of assets from its balance sheet at quarter-ends in 2008 to make leverage look lower; the examiner found colorable claims against senior officers and auditor Ernst & Young. https://jenner.com/lehman/
- Mar 2008: rival Bear Stearns collapsed and was sold to JPMorgan Chase with Federal Reserve support, putting pressure on Lehman. https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/lehman-brothers
- Jun 9, 2008: Lehman reported a second-quarter loss of about $2.8 billion, its first loss as a public company; President Joseph Gregory and CFO Erin Callan were replaced days later. https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-FCIC/pdf/GPO-FCIC.pdf
- Sep 10, 2008: Lehman pre-announced a third-quarter loss of about $3.9 billion after talks with Korea Development Bank failed; its stock collapsed. https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-FCIC/pdf/GPO-FCIC.pdf
- Sep 12-14, 2008: Treasury Secretary Henry Paulson, New York Fed President Timothy Geithner and Fed Chair Ben Bernanke led weekend meetings with Wall Street CEOs at the New York Fed; Bank of America chose to buy Merrill Lynch instead; a Barclays deal failed after UK regulators would not approve it without a shareholder vote; officials declined to provide public money. https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-FCIC/pdf/GPO-FCIC.pdf
- Sep 15, 2008: Lehman Brothers Holdings Inc. filed for Chapter 11 in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York with about $639 billion in assets and $613 billion in debts, the largest bankruptcy filing in U.S. history; about 25,000 employees. Bankruptcy Judge James M. Peck presided. https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/lehman-brothers
- Sep 15, 2008: the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell about 504 points. Sep 16: the Reserve Primary Fund "broke the buck" because of its Lehman holdings; the Fed lent AIG $85 billion. https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/lehman-brothers
- Sep 2008: Barclays bought Lehman's North American investment banking and trading business; Nomura bought its Asia-Pacific and European operations. https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-FCIC/pdf/GPO-FCIC.pdf
- Oct 6, 2008: Fuld testified before the House Committee on Oversight and Government Reform (chair Henry Waxman); Waxman said Fuld had received about $480 million in compensation since 2000; Fuld said he took full responsibility for his decisions and defended them. https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CHRG-110hhrg55767/html/CHRG-110hhrg55767.htm
- Dec 2010: New York Attorney General Andrew Cuomo sued Ernst & Young over its role in Lehman's Repo 105 accounting; E&Y settled in 2015 for $10 million without admitting wrongdoing. No Lehman executives were criminally charged; the SEC did not bring charges against Fuld. https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-FCIC/pdf/GPO-FCIC.pdf
- Mar 6, 2012: Lehman emerged from bankruptcy as a liquidating entity under a confirmed plan and began paying creditors; distributions continued for more than a decade. https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/lehman-brothers
- The Financial Crisis Inquiry Commission (Jan 2011) concluded the crisis was avoidable and cited failures of regulation, corporate governance and risk management. https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-FCIC/pdf/GPO-FCIC.pdf
