- 1983: Silicon Valley Bank founded in Santa Clara, California; became the main bank for U.S. venture-backed startups and their investors; parent SVB Financial Group (Nasdaq: SIVB). https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/files/svb-review-20230428.pdf
- 2019-2021: deposits roughly tripled during the tech boom, from about $62 billion at end-2019 to about $189 billion at end-2021; much of the inflow was invested in long-dated Treasuries and agency mortgage-backed securities (held-to-maturity portfolio). Total assets about $209 billion at end-2022. https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/files/svb-review-20230428.pdf
- Fed review (Vice Chair for Supervision Michael S. Barr, Apr 28, 2023): SVB's board and management failed to manage interest-rate and liquidity risk; supervisors did not fully appreciate the vulnerabilities and did not take sufficient steps; the bank relied heavily on uninsured deposits (about 94% of deposits uninsured at end-2022). https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/files/svb-review-20230428.pdf
- SVB had no permanent chief risk officer for about eight months in 2022 (Laura Izurieta left in April 2022; replacement started January 2023). https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/files/svb-review-20230428.pdf
- 2022: as the Federal Reserve raised interest rates, the market value of SVB's bond portfolio fell; unrealized losses on held-to-maturity securities were about $15 billion at end-2022. https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/files/svb-review-20230428.pdf
- Mar 8, 2023: SVB announced it had sold about $21 billion of available-for-sale securities at a loss of about $1.8 billion and planned to raise about $2.25 billion in capital. https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/files/svb-review-20230428.pdf
- Mar 9, 2023: depositors withdrew about $42 billion in a single day; SVB expected about $100 billion more in withdrawal requests on Mar 10. Shares fell about 60% on Mar 9. https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/files/svb-review-20230428.pdf
- Mar 10, 2023: the California Department of Financial Protection and Innovation (DFPI) closed Silicon Valley Bank and appointed the FDIC as receiver; FDIC created the Deposit Insurance National Bank of Santa Clara. https://www.fdic.gov/news/press-releases/2023/pr23016.html
- Mar 12, 2023: Treasury Secretary Janet Yellen, Fed Chair Jerome Powell and FDIC Chairman Martin Gruenberg announced a systemic risk exception protecting all depositors of SVB and Signature Bank (closed the same day); the Fed created the Bank Term Funding Program (BTFP). https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20230312b.htm
- Mar 13, 2023: FDIC transferred deposits and assets to Silicon Valley Bridge Bank, N.A. https://www.fdic.gov/news/press-releases/2023/pr23019.html
- Mar 17, 2023: parent SVB Financial Group filed for Chapter 11 in the U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Southern District of New York, Case No. 23-10367 (MG), Chief Judge Martin Glenn. https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/719739/000119312524254186/d904756d8k.htm
- Mar 26-27, 2023: First Citizens BancShares (First Citizens Bank) agreed to acquire SVB's deposits and loans; about $72 billion of SVB assets bought at a discount of $16.5 billion; FDIC estimated the cost to the Deposit Insurance Fund at about $20 billion. https://www.fdic.gov/news/press-releases/2023/pr23023.html
- May 16, 2023: former CEO Greg Becker testified before the Senate Banking Committee, blaming the speed of the run and rising rates. https://www.banking.senate.gov/hearings/examining-the-failures-of-silicon-valley-bank-and-signature-bank
- GAO (Apr 28, 2023): SVB and Signature failed due to risky business strategies and weak liquidity and risk management; regulators identified concerns but did not escalate quickly enough. https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-23-106736
- Aug 2, 2024: Judge Martin Glenn confirmed SVB Financial Group's Chapter 11 plan; it became effective Nov 7, 2024. SVB Capital was sold to Pinegrove (backed by Brookfield and Sequoia Heritage). https://restructuring.ra.kroll.com/svbfg/
- Jan 16, 2025: the FDIC, as receiver, sued 17 former SVB officers and directors, including CEO Greg Becker and CFO Daniel Beck, alleging negligence, gross negligence and breach of fiduciary duty and seeking billions of dollars in damages (N.D. Cal. 5:25-cv-00569). These are civil allegations; the defendants deny them. https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.442899/gov.uscourts.cand.442899.1.0.pdf
- Oct 23, 2025: U.S. District Judge Noel Wise (N.D. Cal.) denied the defendants' motions to dismiss; as of October 2026 the case is in active litigation (special master appointed Oct 2, 2026). No criminal charges have been filed against SVB executives. https://business.cch.com/BFLD/FDIC-v-Becker-NDCal-Order-Denying-MTD-ECF-140-10232025.pdf
- SVB was at the time the second-largest bank failure in U.S. history (later described by the FDIC as the third-largest after First Republic). https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cand.442899/gov.uscourts.cand.442899.1.0.pdf
